Supermarket Sales Analysis: Driving customer loyalty

Scenario

The company aims to increase customer loyalty by identifying the cities that generate the most sales and provide the best customer experience. Management plans to use these insights to design targeted loyalty programmes and improve overall customer satisfaction.

Download & view project file here The dataset used for this project can be found here

Data Attributes Analysed

  • Location: City (Mandalay, Naypyitaw, Yangon)
  • Customer Information: Customer Type (Member/Normal), Gender
  • Product Categories: Electronic accessories, Fashion accessories, Food and beverages, Health and beauty, Home and lifestyle, Sports and travel
  • Customer Experience: Rating Categories (Poor, Bad, Okay, Good, Great)

Analysis Goals

  • ✅ Which branch/city performs the best with sales?
  • ✅ How do sales compare with members vs normal customers?
  • ✅ What trends exist across different genders?
  • ✅ What are the purchase behaviours of customers?
  • ✅ What is the relationship between customer satisfaction and customer type?

Methods Used

  • Tool: Microsoft Excel
  • Data cleaning & preparation:
    - Standardised date and number format
    - Categorised ratings for clearer analysis
    - Checked for & handled any missing values
  • Pivot Tables: Generated visual insights
  • Dashboard: Interactive dashboard with slicers for further filtering to visualise findings

Key Findings

  • 📊 Naypyitaw makes the most sales with approximately R56,881 in total sales.
  • 📊 Total sales by members (R164,222) and normal customers (R158,741) are almost evenly split
    (~51% and ~49% respectively).
  • 📊 The female customers generally show higher spending in Naypyitaw while the male customers show strongest spending in Mandalay & Yangon.
    The female customers contribute the most to the overall sales (roughly R167,882 in total).
  • 📊 There are consistent sales among all the product lines with both member and normal customers showing similar spending behaviour.
    Members spent more on food and beverages whilst normal customers spent more on electronic accessories.
  • 📊 The branches have a similar average rating (ranging from 6.8 - 7.1) despite Naypyitaw making significantly more (R40,000) in sales.
  • 📊 Most ratings customer ratings are positive (majority being 'okay' or 'good').

Conclusion

  • Investigate Naypyitaw's high-performance strategies and apply them to the branches in Mandalay and Yangon.
  • Have more campaigns that target male customers.
  • Promote membership benefits to leverage spending from member customers.
  • Create targeted programs to convert more normal customers to members.
  • Investigate Naypyitaw's ratings compared to its high sales.
  • Focus on turning 'Okay' ratings to 'Good' and 'Great'.
  • Find out why customers give a 'Bad' rating to prevent future customer dissatisfaction.
